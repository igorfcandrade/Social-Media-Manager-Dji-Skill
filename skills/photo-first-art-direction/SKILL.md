## Context and ownership

Apply `../social-media-manager/references/contexto-do-caso.md`: receive context from the message, attachments, connected sources, or existing project documents without requiring a particular filename or format. When a project provides a manifest or source index, follow its canonical sources, existing approvals, and storage locations. Brand-specific skills and approved brand rules take precedence in their scope. Keep proposals separate from approved rules; use the project’s existing documents instead of creating parallel sources. Social objectives and the current production plan come from `../social-media-manager/references/04-criacao-de-conteudo.md` and the project context.

Create a reusable visual direction in which photography carries most of the identity and graphic elements remain subordinate. Adapt every recommendation to the brand, audience, production capacity, channels, and available equipment.

## Establish the brief

Identify or infer, without inventing facts:

- brand, offer, audience, market, channels, personality, positioning, and central idea;
- existing palette, typography, logo rules, photography, and references;
- the current visual problem and the desired balance between photography and graphics;
- practical constraints such as phone capture, natural light, location, budget, and who produces the content.

Flag assumptions that would materially change the direction. If evidence is missing, make the smallest useful assumption and label it.

## Research from real sources

When research is requested, prioritise current first-party brand pages, campaigns, product pages, editorial pages, and public social posts. Use aggregators, trend articles, stock libraries, and inspiration platforms only as secondary discovery aids.

For each concrete reference, record the brand or source, direct URL, consultation date, format, objective composition, why it works, what transfers, and what should not be copied. State access limitations instead of claiming to have inspected inaccessible accounts, feeds, images, or metrics.

Separate:

- transferable visual principles;
- distinctive codes belonging to the source brand;
- short-lived trends;
- decisions realistic for the commissioning brand.

Do not infer performance from visual appearance. Do not describe unseen imagery.

## Analyse the visual system

Compare at least the dimensions relevant to the brief:

- ratio of photography to visible text;
- light direction, softness, contrast, white balance, and colour treatment;
- camera distance, angle, crop, negative space, product scale, and hierarchy;
- backgrounds, surfaces, props, hands, people, styling, and material texture;
- logo frequency, text quantity, placement, and legibility;
- feed rhythm and differences between product, process, campaign, informational, and video content.

Elegance must be explained through observable decisions, not vague labels such as “premium”, “clean”, or “editorial”.

## Define an executable direction

Translate the findings into a system the actual team can produce. Specify:

- five core art-direction principles;
- five recurring mistakes to avoid;
- a concrete photography system covering light, backgrounds, distances, angles, props, colour treatment, capture, and selection;
- content families with their purpose, required photography, maximum on-image text, and use cases;
- a sample nine-post rhythm that alternates scale, density, people, product, and visual breathing room;
- a handoff brief with fixed rules, flexible choices, accessibility checks, and approval criteria.

For small teams using phones, favour repeatable physical conditions over expensive equipment: one reliable light source, a small background set, stable camera positions, native vertical capture, consistent exposure, and restrained editing.

## Protect the photo-first principle

Do not use decorative graphics to rescue weak photography. Treat typography, palette, logo, borders, shapes, and calls to action as a quiet support layer. Keep negative space photographic whenever possible rather than manufacturing it with opaque boxes.

Do not create templates or finished posts during a research-and-direction phase. End by listing the decisions that require approval before design begins.

## Deliverables and portability

Write in the language requested by the user. Prefer portable outputs: a concise README, an operational Markdown brief, a full research document, and a structured references table. Add PDF or spreadsheet versions only when useful.
